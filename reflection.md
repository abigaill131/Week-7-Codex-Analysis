# Week 7: Codex CLI Reflection

My analytical question was to understand how home sale prices differed across neighborhoods in Ames, Iowa. I started by asking Codex to explore the dataset and recommend an approach before producing any outputs. This allowed me to understand the dataset more, review its initial findings, and understand how it was going to approach the analysis rather than just immediately accepting a completed result.

One issue I encountered was an inconsistency in codex's missing-value summary. It reported 510 blank cells overall, but two individual variables had combined 649 missing values. After noticing this discrepancy, I asked Codex to recalculate the missing values and distinguish blank cells from "NA" values. It corrected the total to 719. 

Overall I think my prompts became more specific overtime. The first prompt focused on understanding the dataset and receiving recommendations. In the second prompt, I established how I wanted the analysis performed (like using median sale price as the primary comparison, retaining outliers, and limiting the visualization to neighborhoods with at least 20 sales). After reviewing the initial chart SVG generated, I noticed several opportunities to improve how it looked visually. My third prompt requested specific changes, including removing gridlines, centering the title, changing the sorting order, and removing unnecessary decimal places. The final chart was much easier to interpret.

While working with codex, what surprised me was how most of the work involved evaluating the outputs and making decisions rather than writing code. Codex did the analysis itself very quickly and I spent a majority of the time assessing whether the results, its assumptions, and the visualization were appropriate. If I completed this assignment again, I would establish clearer guidelines for creating the visualization earlier to reduce the need for revisions. Overall, this assignment helped me better understand how to direct Codex AI through an iterative process while maintaining responsibility for the major decisions and final results/outputs.
